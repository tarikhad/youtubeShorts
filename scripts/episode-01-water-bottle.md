## Script

On-screen text carries the script. Narration lines are kept for a later voiceover.

**[0-4 s] HOOK**
- Visual: small pellets fall from the top of the frame.
- On-screen text: "This bottle didn't start as a bottle."

**[4-10 s] SETUP**
- Visual: the pellets settle into a pile.
- On-screen text: "It started as tiny plastic pellets." Sub-line: "PET resin, made from ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid" [S1, S2]

**[10-18 s] STEP 1**
- Visual: the pellet pile melts into a small test-tube shape.
- On-screen text: "Step 1: molded into a 'preform'" [S3]

**[18-28 s] STEP 2**
- Visual: the preform glows orange, then inflates into the bottle shape. The neck stays the same size.
- On-screen text: "Step 2: heated, then blown into shape with air" Sub-line: "Above ~100 °C" [S3, S4]

**[28-36 s] TURN**
- Visual: a "Bottle maker" box sends a pallet of empty bottles to a separate "Water plant" box.
- On-screen text: "Twist: many water plants don't make their bottles. They arrive empty, on pallets." [S5, S6]

**[36-46 s] PAYOFF**
- Visual: the bottle moves along a line of stations: rinse, fill, cap, label, pack. Water fills it at the fill station.
- On-screen text: "Then: rinse → fill → cap → label → pack" [S6, S7]

**[46-52 s] DISTRIBUTION**
- Visual: a truck icon carries the pack to a store shelf.
- On-screen text: "Then a truck to a store shelf." [S8]

**[52-55 s] LOOP**
- Visual: the bottle fades and pellets begin to fall again, matching the first frame.
- On-screen text: "So where did yours start?"

Narration (for a later voiceover, about 90 words): "This bottle didn't start as a bottle. It started as tiny plastic pellets. First, they're molded into a small tube called a preform. Then the preform is heated and blown into shape with air. Here's the twist: many water plants don't make their bottles at all. They arrive empty, on pallets. Then they're rinsed, filled, capped, labeled, and packed, and a truck takes them to the shelf. So where did yours start?"

**Note on "many" in the turn:** S5 and S6 are two documented plants that receive bottles from outside suppliers. They show the practice exists; they do not measure how common it is. "Many" is a reasonable reading of two independent trade-press examples, but it is the weakest claim in the video. Safer wording if the owner prefers: "Some water plants don't make their bottles."
